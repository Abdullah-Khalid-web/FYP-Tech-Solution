48	Heading 1	ABSTRACT
49	Normal	In Pakistan, small and medium sized shops continue to utilise paper records, memory and stock counts done by guesswork, sales are difficult to track and the owners are seldom aware of which products are making them profits. Current software is costly, designed and targeted towards big companies and is useless once the internet drops. This thesis features ManageHub which is a web first, cloud based Software as a Service (SaaS) which configures inventory, sales, customers, suppliers, staff and finance into a single system which can be accessed by all via a browser with no installation. An offline capable mode is supported by a special Electron desktop client that continues to operate in the event of an internet outage and synchronizes back upon connection. The Node.js Express server supports a multi tenant MySQL database, allowing multiple shops to safely share the system, without sharing their data. A shopkeeper with a large language model, FastAI, and LangChain using a plain language assistant may hypothesize, ask particular questions (e.g., how much sugar is left) or make commands (e.g., add two crates of soft drinks). Sensitive actions are the ones that must always be human-approved and role-based access control restricts every user to what he or she must view. The system was developed in resistance to a formal Software Requirement Specification and was module tested online and offline providing the locals with a much cheaper, easier to use, and more locally relevant alternative to a costly foreign ERP software.

77	Normal	Enter any small Pakistani shop a grocer, a bakery, a hardware store, a pharmacy and the trend continues, a handwritten record book, a calculator and an owner who will tell who is in debt. Adding the figures in sales is not only time-consuming and prone to errors but also requires the use of hand, salaries and loans of the staff are managed on a piece of paper and it is difficult to detect any mistakes. Since all is recorded in a single notebook or memory of a single person, the individual that owns it will have little opportunity to figure out what products sell, who the customers are that are worth keeping and where the money is possibly being squandered.

125	Heading 2	1.5 Why a Web Based, Offline Capable Platform
126	Normal	One intentional architectural choice made in ManageHub is the intention to use the web browser as the main means of accessing the system, instead of a native application, which will have to be installed and serviced on all the tills. This clearly has drawbacks to a small retailer: nothing needs to be installed to allow a new cashier to be immediately made active; all the updates can be centralised, allowing all the shops to have a patch or added feature the moment they refresh the page; and since the pages are generated by the server as normal HTML, the interface becomes consistent across older, re-purposed hardware.
127	Normal	The only weakness a purely web based system has is that when the connection fails, a window in a browser has nothing to revert to, something that a shop that is not able to afford to discontinue selling cannot tolerate. ManageHub handles this by providing an Electron desktop client (Chapter 3) that uses the same backend and data model but includes a local database and a background sync engine to allow billing and stock updates to continue even when there is no connection whatsoever and automatically resynchronizes with the cloud when the connection is re-established. The default usage (and constant up-to-date method) is the web dashboard, used at all times - the desktop client is used when the internet is untrustworthy.

130	Heading 2	2.1 Introduction
131	Normal	All the in-between steps leading up to the creation of ManageHub made sense to research the current state of how businesses managing retail operations are run today via notebook and calculator to enterprise software to current AI-enhanced cloud solutions. This chapter tours that spectrum, focusing especially on the methods by which each of them is accessed over the web and how they perform under circumstances in which the internet is unreliable, in which each fails a small Pakistani retailer, and in which ManageHub fits.
132	Heading 2	2.2 Manual and Paper Based Retail Management
133	Normal	The simplest method, which is in operation in the majority of small stores, is a paper book, with a calculator or simple spreadsheet. It is free and requires no training, but is not scaled: no automatic tracking of stock, no low-stock alert, no intuitive look at what products provide any profit and all reports are manually constructed. Errors are usual and maintaining consistency of numbers becomes a real trial at the point when a shop exceeds one location or shift. Paradoxically, even a paper ledger is the only system in this site that can never go offline as a reminder that any digital substitution must consider connectivity loss as a serious issue instead of closing their eyes to it.
135	Heading 2	2.3 Off the Shelf Point of Sale and ERP Systems
136	Normal	On the opposite end of the scorecard is in place the established products like Odoo, ERPNext, Square, and QuickBooks Point of Sale mature, feature rich products targeting a large, usually Western market. Odoo and ERPNext are open source and versatile, however, that versatility requires a developer to customize modules, integrate local payment options and localize the interface, which is significantly beyond the abilities of a small shop owner to do so on their own. Square and QuickBooks POS are well polished yet its price, currency and offline behaviour are predicated on secure internet and card first payments which is not necessarily applicable in the world of the Pakistani retail industry. None of these systems are modeled based on the Urdu-first or the combination of cash, informal credit, and mobile wallets usage in local stores.
137	Heading 2	2.4 Cloud and SaaS Retail Platforms
138	Normal	Software-as-a-Service model where one pays a subscription fee rather than purchase and install software has become the most common form of delivering business software as it eliminates the requirement of a shop to maintain its own server and only the vendor can push out an update in a central location. This is affordable at scale as it uses a shared platform (including many customers but maintaining the isolation of their data) and the infrastructure cost of a single store is shared across all locations instead of being repeated per location. ManageHub adheres to this pattern, sharing a common Node.js and MySQL back-end, in which all records have a scope to a single shop, keeping running expenses minimal to make the service available at a price that can be offered to small retailers.
140	Heading 2	2.5 Session Based Web Applications vs. Installed Software
141	Normal	One such pattern in business software design is whether to implement a thick client that is installed on a computer, a thin client that is purely in a browser and requires server-side rendering and session-based authentication. Installed software can be faster to feel, operating offline in the default configuration, but is more difficult to update, and confer security with numerous machines. Web applications that are server-rendered, that is, where the server constructs each page (as opposed to a single-page application where the logic of interpreting user-generated input is kept on the client), are easier to reason about and maintain consistency among, as its interface logic is only needed to run anywhere. The web dashboard of ManageHub is based on this server-rendered, session-based patterning, and it is only that a true, distinct installed client (the Electron desktop application) is essentially needed when there is real need off-line persistence between it and a browser.
142	Heading 2	2.6 Artificial Intelligence in Retail: Forecasting and Recommendations
143	Normal	Machine learning has been extensively applied in the retail industry to forecast demand, recommend reorder, and raise flags (typically by analyzing past sales data), and convert the forecast into a recommended activity e.g. order 20 items. Stock Agent and Forecast Agent by ManageHub pursue this concept, but put a human in the loop: the system is a recommendation to place an order, and a manager either accepts it or modifies it and retains the advantage of data-driven recommendations without letting computers make financial choices.
145	Heading 2	2.7 Conversational and Agentic AI Assistants
146	Normal	Over the past few years, there has been a transition to agentic AI systems (matching key word in chatbots) along with large language models (LLMs) understanding an unstructured request, considering internal tools that will be involved and responding or acting accordingly. This scheme of an intent classifier or router anteceding specialised agents is now typical in AI-assisted business software, and what ManageHub agentic AI service adheres to, where LangChain is used to coordinate agents and a hosted LLM (via Groq) to understand language. The moral of the story always of this work is that only a confirmatory action ( худож , ) can prevent an irreparable event happening within the channel outside of human control otherwise known as human in the loop design, implemented by ManageHub to its billing and stock reorder agents.
147	Heading 2	2.8 Offline First and Local First Software
148	Normal	Applications which require offline functionality tend to be designed following a local-first pattern: the app writes data to a local database as it leaves the app and pushes changes that need to be sent to the server onto the queue, and syncs the queue in the background upon an available connection. The shared-record two-device conflicts When offline or conflicting with a device that is offline are usually solved with timestamps or a simple last-write-wins rule, a pragmatic compromise between full consistency and full availability. The Electron desktop client of ManageHub conforms to this trend perfectly: all operations are initialised to a local SQLite database from which they are then queued and sent to a shared MySQL database when the internet is available again, with the version of operations prevailing based on the updated at timestamps. This offline functionality layers on top of the same backend and schema as the browser dashboard that a shop can hop between browser and desktop and always view the same data after being synced.

165	Normal	This chapter describes the design and construction of ManageHub: the development framework, general architecture, technology selection of each layer, data share design, web application construction and access control, and the architecture of the web application, as well as the two distinguishing elements offline-first synchronisation and agentic AI assistant.
166	Heading 2	3.2 Development Approach
167	Normal	This project did not follow a single plan when developing but as an iterative process. The team started with the modules any shop requires on day one products, inventory, and billing and overlaid on top of these modules the HR and payroll, raw materials, offline sync, and the agentic AI assistant and tested each of the modules and its needs before proceeding on to the next. The entire process was done with Git and the codebase was structured into distinct layers (routes, controllers, views, helpers, middleware) in such a manner that a new module can be added without affecting others. Due to the web dashboard being the core product out of the initial iteration, all subsequent features such as the offline desktop client built upon the same backend (as well as schema) and not as an independent platform maintained by manual syncing.
169	Heading 2	3.3 System Architecture Overview
170	Normal	ManageHub is a layered based system, displayed in figure 1. There are two options to get to the system: a regular web browser, the default, which has no installation to follow, and an Electron-based desktop application as shops might need a guaranteed offline connection at the till. They both communicate to the same Node.js/Express backend via a REST API, and both are used to authenticate and use one of their sessions and do business-level logic. In addition, it is accompanied by a separate agentic AI service, developed using FastAPI, which the backend and browser can request to, when making a natural-language or voice request. Both write to and read out of one, multi-tenant MySQL database that is safely accessed by stores. The client also maintains a local SQLite cache enabling it to continue to function in the event that the path to MySQL is inaccessible.

185	Heading 2	3.6 Web Application Design
186	Normal	Due to the use of the browser as the main tool of ManageHub, some attention was given to the construction of the web application. The server-side is rendered with EJS templates, written into on shared layout and on reusable partials a header, navigation bar and role-based partial sidebar to ensure that once a single change to the navigation or branding is made, it matches across the entire site immediately. Authentication is implemented with express-session that is supported by a MySQL session store and therefore, the identity of a logged-in user is stored not in a browser-controlled token, but on the server and in its session, which only includes a user ID and a role, and which is again validated against the database store with each request.
187	Normal	A request moves through a set of relatively predictable middlewares, and then to a controller: session parsing, CSRF verification of anything that modifies data, rate limiting access to sensitive routes, including login, and the role-based access control (RBAC) discussed in Section 3.8. When there is a successful save, a validation problem, a permission problem it is surfaced via express-flash messages at the start of the subsequent page, not using a discrete client-side notification mechanism. Due to the entire interface being rendered like this, a shop can and will be able to open ManageHub in any modern day browser, on any computer, and be presented with the same fully featured dashboard, without needing to install anything, or maintain it locally.

215	Heading 2	3.8 Role-Based Access Control
216	Normal	Since a shop has multiple types of users whom an owner, a manager, an accountant, and an HR staff, an inventory manager, and cashiers ManageHub implements role-based access control (RBAC) on middleware as referenced in Figure 3. Any request is verified with the user role and, in some cases an additional control (related to the actual permission) should be requested (e.g. shop.view or shop.edit). Roles with wider authority like Shop Owner or Super Admin automatically bypass all checks, whereas more limited roles, like needing a cashier to process a bill but not open payroll; an HR manager to manage employees but not global options. Anyone without a login, or outside their role is redirected to the login page or is presented with an unambiguous access denied page.

221	Heading 2	3.9 Offline-First Desktop App and Data Synchronisation
222	Normal	Pakistan still does not have the best internet connectivity everywhere which is why, together with the web dashboard, ManageHub includes an Electron desktop client developed to stay on even at full offline. This section explains the way that that offline capability is implemented.
223	Heading 3	3.9.1 Local Database and Sync Queue
224	Normal	All operations that a cashier performs on the desktop app are logged straight to a local SQLite database (through sql.js) and at the same time to a sync queue table. The cashier does not wait to get the cloud: the local write is immediate and the network route is served completely in the background.
227	Heading 3	3.9.2 Background Sync Service
228	Normal	One of them is a background sync service that regularly examines their ability to connect to the internet of the shop and the shared MySQL server (approximately every minute). Otherwise, the app will continue to operate not connected and the queue will continue to expand securely; otherwise, once the service is available, the queue will be emptied to update changes to be uploaded and download newer records created in other devices.
229	Heading 3	3.9.3 Conflict Resolution
230	Normal	Since multiple devices can be offline, and changing data simultaneously, the sync engine must have a rule of which version of a record should prevail. Each table version at ManageHub uses the value of the updated-at column: the last version in a database is considered the authoritative one under an honest and straightforward rule: the last version updated in a single shift is the one behave a single shop is expected to use in a single shift, but not a complete field-by-field merge, as described in Section 5.6.
231	Heading 3	3.9.4 Direct-to-MySQL Sync Mode
232	Normal	The sync service will be able to conveniently connect to the MySQL database directly of the shop instead of via the REST API in Electron mode, clearing the pending backlog by batch instead of per tick, meaning that a big backlog due to a lengthy offline period does not require an unreasonable number of cycles to clear. The records are automatically translated as they are moved between two databases with UUID values changing their binary MySQL representation into their string counterpart and vice versa.
234	Heading 3	3.9.5 Keeping the User Informed
235	Normal	Since a cashier should know whether he/she is working offline or not, the desktop app exposes the state of sync, right in the interface (offlineService.js, syncStatus.js) indicating whether it is online, syncing, has changes pending, or is entirely offline making what would otherwise be a background process of no immediate utility to a non-technical user to appear trusted.

238	Heading 2	3.10 Agentic AI Assistant
239	Normal	Part of the distinguishing feature of ManageHub is an agentic AI helper, which is an independent FastAPI service, that allows a shopkeeper to use plain text to interact with the system, rather than clicking through a menu. A query like What is the total sale today? or Add two crates of soft drinks will first be sent through an intent classifier on the open list with key word matching and possibly an ambiguous query get them run through a large language model (via LangChain and Groq). A request is then passed to an agent router which in turn passes the request to one of seven specialised agents which make calls to the same backend REST API that the web dashboard accepts and, as such, none can circumvent the usual business rules of the system. This is indicated in Figure 5.
241	Normal	Figure 5: How the Agentic AI Assistant Handles a Request
242	Heading 3	3.10.1 Retail Assistant Agent
243	Normal	Answers general questions about sales and inventory (how much stock of a product is left or what the total sales are on a specific day).
246	Heading 3	3.10.2 Billing Agent
247	Normal	Drives voice-based billing: It describes a verbal order such as add two crates of soft drinks, creates the related sale and it just waits until the cashier replies before it writes anything to its database.
248	Heading 3	3.10.3 Stock (Ordering) Agent
249	Normal	Compares the existing stock levels with the sales history and generates a draft reorder recommendation, a manager has to review it and expressly approve, modify, or disapprove before a purchase order is generated.
250	Heading 3	3.10.4 Forecast Agent
251	Normal	Produces uncomplicated sales and demand details of a product or category to enable a manager to predict what will sell off well in the next few days.
252	Heading 3	3.10.5 Anomaly Agent
253	Normal	Identifies the transactions which are not typical of the usual trends of a shop, alerting a manager of its existence instead of acting on them automatically.
254	Heading 3	3.10.6 Staff Agent
255	Normal	Makes plain-language summaries of staff performance in a specified period of time, which the user can search by.
258	Heading 3	3.10.7 Report Agent
259	Normal	Makes simple a typical report by making a small, narrated, plain language summary, e.g., by explaining what changed in this week in sales report instead of delivering a table of numbers which do not make a lot of sense.
260	Normal	Since certain agents may work on real money or stocks, all sensitive actions provide a human-in-the-loop verification measure as detailed above in the Billing and Stock Agents that retain the convenience of an AI assistant without eliminating the final decision of a human.
261	Heading 2	3.11 Security Measures
262	Normal	There were several common security practices implemented over the system:
263	List Paragraph	Passwords have not been reported to be saved in plaintext; hashing with bcrypt was done and then they are saved.
264	List Paragraph	User sessions are saved on the server through the use of express session backed up by a MySQL session store as opposed to relying on the data posted by the client.
265	List Paragraph	Forms: Cross-site request forgery (CSRF) is guarded with the use of the csurf middleware.
266	List Paragraph	Pricey endpoints such as the ones used to log in are rate limited to delay brute force intrusions.
267	List Paragraph	All of the secured paths are traversed by the role based access control middleware of Section 3.8, prior to data actually being sent out.
268	List Paragraph	The local database on the desktop application is merely a temporary cache and a synchronisation queue to minimise the amount of sensitive information stored unprotected in a single store.
269	List Paragraph	Since the web dashboard is provided on the server side, all access rules are applied in a single place that can be audited, as opposed to client-side logic and API logic having the same conditions over and over again.

418	Normal	CHAPTER 6
419	Heading 1	6. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
420	Heading 2	6.1 Conclusion
421	Normal	Although software that can help in this can often be too costly, complicated, or reliant on always having internet access, small and medium retail shops in Pakistan are largely operating on paper, memory and guesswork. ManageHub bridges that divide: a single, Web-based, cloud-based SaaS solution that unites inventory, sales, customers, suppliers, employees, and finance, and can be accessed via any browser with no software to install, and complements by Electron desktop client in any shop that requires assured offline connectivity when at the till.
422	Normal	It was developed with a well-written Software Requirement Specification and structured note, behind a formal Node.js/MySQL (backend) interface, a server-rendered web app archived as a single (continuously published) deployment, with user access based on role, and an Electron desktop client that operates offline and auto-syncs, and an agentic AI service that allows a shopkeeper to ask questions or to bill a sale in plain language, and a dedicated agentic AI service that oversees any action touching money or stock. The testing of the system in all modules, roles and online/ offline conditions demonstrated the system to act as desired and achieve its objectives.
423	Normal	Through the brief explanation, it was proved that nowadays SaaS and AI solutions could expand and cover the market segment that small retailers in the area typically did not enter into that dialogue, but it did not necessarily mean a trade-off between the cost, languages, the number of installation efforts, or the reliability.
424	Heading 2	6.2 Future Work
425	Normal	Although ManageHub is already a solution with the basic requirements to support a small retail store, it can be expanded in a few directions:
426	List Paragraph	Creating a special-purpose, Android and iOS application. The mobile-based web dashboard can already work with a mobile browser, but an offline-caching native app with push notifications would be appropriate to owners who would like to check their business using a phone.
427	List Paragraph	Load and performance testing- in a realistic multi tenant load in order to verify the scalability objectives proposed in the non-functional requirements.
428	List Paragraph	Greater, AI-supported prediction, eating a greater range of past data to enhance reorder and demand predictions with time.
429	List Paragraph	Direct integrations to e-commerce and marketplace, hence the online and in-store inventory of a shop is kept up to date.
430	List Paragraph	A more detailed tax and GST interface of areas with official requirements of e-invoicing.
431	List Paragraph	More integration of hardware, e.g., first class support of barcode scanners, receipt printers, and card terminals.
432	List Paragraph	Better accessibility (such as text-to-speech) and more languages on top of English and Urdu.
433	List Paragraph	More progressive conflict resolution mechanism of offline sync, beyond last-write-wins, e.g., field-level merging of records being edited on several machines when they are offline.
434	List Paragraph	Proper security audit, penetration test prior to broader commercial use.
435	List Paragraph	The web dashboard will have Progressive Web App (PWA) support, which will install an icon in the browser and provide a limited offline cache to include those shops with isolated offline connectivity currently.
436	Heading 2	6.3 Final Remarks

Through the above improvements, ManageHub can have a feasible approach to becoming a real-life, daily use application by small retailers in Pakistan.
